How to Run a Free AI Assistant 24/7 on a $300 Chromebook

February 22, 2026 · 7 min read

Most guides about running personal AI agents assume you have a beefy Mac, a Linux server, or a paid VPS. But what if you have a $300 Chromebook sitting on your desk?

Turns out, that's more than enough. I've been running a 24/7 AI assistant on a Chromebook for weeks now. It monitors my email, posts to my blog, does research, and messages me on Telegram when something needs my attention. Here's exactly how to do it.

What You'll Need

  • Any Chromebook (literally any model — even a $200 one works)
  • Power adapter (keep it plugged in)
  • A free OpenRouter account for AI model access
  • A Telegram account (free)
  • About 30 minutes to set up

Step 1: Enable Linux on Your Chromebook

ChromeOS has a built-in Linux container called Crostini. It's free, runs silently in the background, and survives sleep/wake cycles.

  1. Open Settings → search for "Linux"
  2. Click "Turn on" and follow the prompts
  3. Wait about 5 minutes for it to install

Once done, you'll have a full Debian Linux terminal available. This is where your AI agent will live.

Step 2: Keep the Chromebook Awake

By default, ChromeOS suspends the Linux container when the screen turns off — which kills your agent. Fix this first:

  1. Go to Settings → Device → Power
  2. Set "When idle, sleep" to Never
  3. Keep it plugged into power

That's it. Your Linux container will now run indefinitely.

Step 3: Install OpenClaw

OpenClaw is an open-source AI agent framework that runs locally and connects to Telegram. It's what makes this whole setup work.

Open your Linux terminal and run:

npm install -g openclaw
openclaw configure
openclaw gateway start

During configuration, you'll paste in your OpenRouter API key (free to get at openrouter.ai) and connect your Telegram bot. The setup wizard walks you through both.

🔗 OpenClaw: openclaw.ai — free and open source. Full docs at docs.openclaw.ai.

Step 4: Connect Telegram

Telegram is how you talk to your agent from anywhere — your phone, another computer, wherever. Setup takes 2 minutes:

  1. Message @BotFather on Telegram
  2. Send /newbot and follow the prompts
  3. Copy the bot token it gives you
  4. Paste it into OpenClaw's configuration

Now you can message your AI from your phone and it responds — even when your Chromebook screen is off.

Step 5: Pick a Cheap AI Model

OpenClaw connects to AI models via OpenRouter. The cheapest reliable options:

  • Gemini 2.0 Flash — ~$0.10 per million tokens. Fast, capable, barely costs anything for normal use.
  • DeepSeek V3 — ~$0.27 per million tokens. Excellent at coding and research tasks.
  • Claude Haiku — ~$0.80 per million tokens. Best for nuanced writing.

For context: a normal conversation uses maybe 5,000-10,000 tokens. At Gemini Flash prices, you'd spend about $0.001 per conversation. Running it 24/7 for a month costs less than a coffee.

What Can It Actually Do?

Once running, your AI agent can:

  • Read and send emails on your behalf
  • Search the web and summarize what it finds
  • Monitor your GitHub repos and alert you to issues
  • Write and publish blog posts
  • Run shell commands on your Chromebook
  • Message you proactively when something needs attention
  • Check your calendar and remind you of upcoming events

The key difference from just using ChatGPT: this runs continuously. You don't have to open a browser and start a conversation. It's working in the background 24/7, reaching out to you when it finds something worth your attention.

The Real Cost

Here's the breakdown for running this 24/7 for a month:

  • Hardware: Chromebook you already own (or $200-300 new)
  • Electricity: ~$1-2/month (Chromebooks are very power efficient)
  • AI model costs: $2-5/month for normal usage on Gemini Flash
  • OpenClaw: Free
  • Telegram: Free

Total: ~$3-7/month after the hardware. That's cheaper than every AI subscription out there, and you own the whole stack.

Limitations to Know

This setup isn't perfect. A few things to be aware of:

  • No browser automation: Chromebook's Linux container can't run a GUI browser headlessly, so tasks requiring clicking through websites are limited.
  • Chromebook-specific: If ChromeOS updates force a restart, you'll need to manually restart the gateway (or set it up as a systemd service, which OpenClaw supports).
  • Not for heavy compute: AI inference happens in the cloud via OpenRouter, so your Chromebook's CPU doesn't matter much. But local heavy processing isn't an option.

Is It Worth It?

If you've been curious about AI agents but didn't want to pay $20-100/month for managed services, this is the answer. You get a fully capable, always-on AI assistant for the cost of a few coffees per month, running on hardware you might already own.

The Chromebook angle is the hidden gem here. It's silent, energy-efficient, cheap, and ChromeOS's Linux support has gotten genuinely good. It's not a compromise — it's a surprisingly solid foundation for a personal AI stack.
